What the ChFC and CLU Designations Mean for Financial Planning Clients in Greater Boston

📅 August 12, 2026
✎ 4-Point Financial Editorial Team
⌚ 4 min read

Financial planning credentials vary widely in rigor, and prospective clients rarely have an easy way to tell them apart. The Chartered Financial Consultant (ChFC) and Chartered Life Underwriter (CLU) designations that David Keefe holds are among the more demanding options in the field.

Both designations are awarded through The American College of Financial Services and require multi-course study covering insurance, estate planning, taxation, and retirement strategy. For a Waltham-based advisor like David Keefe, holding both signals a breadth of technical training beyond baseline licensing requirements.

What Each Designation Covers

The ChFC curriculum centers on comprehensive financial planning: cash flow, investment strategy, insurance, and retirement and estate considerations, examined together rather than in isolation.

The CLU designation, historically the insurance industry's flagship credential, focuses more specifically on risk management, insurance planning, and the estate and business planning issues insurance is often used to address.

Precise credentials, not general claims, are what let a prospective client evaluate an advisor before ever picking up the phone.

Why This Matters for Greater Boston Families

David Keefe pairs these designations with FINRA Series 6 and Series 63 registration and licensing as a Life and Health Advisor in Massachusetts and more than 20 additional states, a combination that supports the elder care and long-term care planning work that has become a focus of his practice.

For families in Waltham, Newton, and Watertown evaluating a financial planner, that licensing and credential record is verifiable public information rather than a marketing claim, and it can be checked directly through FINRA and state licensing databases.

Credentials as a Foundation, Not a Headline

Keefe's 32-year production record with New York Life, which places him among the top 100 of approximately 12,000 agents nationwide, developed alongside these designations rather than instead of them.

That combination of longevity, verified production, and formal credentialing is part of what he brings to his work at 4-Point Financial, LLC, the Waltham practice he founded and leads.

Conclusion

For clients across Greater Boston, the ChFC and CLU designations are a starting point for understanding David Keefe's qualifications, not a substitute for reviewing his written answers on Quora or speaking with him directly.
